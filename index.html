How it works
target gaze (dx, dy, dz) └─▶ flat RGB map   R=(dx+1)/2   G=(dy+1)/2   B=(dz+1)/2 └─▶ VAE encode ──▶ InstantX ControlNet · TRAINED · 1768M └─ 5 residuals ─▶ Qwen-Image-Edit-2509 · FROZEN ──▶ output source photo ────────────────────────────────▶ in-context reference token (carries identity) text embedding = zero — every control signal arrives through the ControlNet

The condition is a single flat colour, so it says which way to face and nothing about where anything is. That is the point: the claim under test is that a large pretrained editing model already holds the head-pose ↔ body-pose prior, and the adapter only has to index it. No skeleton, no pose predictor, no text prompt.

Every image here: 25 source photographs × 12 yaw × 5 pitch = 1500 generations, one checkpoint (cn_stage2_step040000) at 1024², cn_scale 1.0, 16 denoising steps, one fixed seed — so differences across a grid come from the gaze condition alone. Ten of the sources are photographs the model has never seen.

Read these with the caveat that the instruments are unreliable. Direction is measured by a head-pose estimator (6DRepNet360), which disagrees with eye-gaze estimators by a median 26.7° on real photographs; id_sim is ArcFace cosine, averaged only over generations where a face was detected at all — which excludes the back-facing cases where identity is hardest. Both this model and its alternative sit near ArcFace's own same-person threshold (≈0.28), so identity preservation is marginal, not solved. The pictures carry more evidence than the numbers.

What is honest to say about this

Zero-shot identities degrade but do not collapse. Large rotations are where both direction accuracy and identity are weakest. Supervision is synthetic — turnaround videos generated with Wan2.2 — which holds identity fixed by construction but bakes that generator's artifacts into the targets. Facial detail is dominated by resolution, not by the model: the same weights and seed render an eye across 25 px or 106 px depending only on framing, a finding that arrived after two rounds of loss engineering had already been spent on it. The black bands on some outputs are letterbox from padding portrait photographs to a square canvas; they are left visible rather than cropped away. The repository documents the failures alongside the results.
