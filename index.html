GazeCtrl

Turn a person in a photograph to face any direction — head and body, through the full 360° — driven by a single 3-vector. No skeleton, no pose predictor, no text prompt. The gaze vector is the condition.

How it works

target gaze (dx, dy, dz) └─▶ flat RGB map   R=(dx+1)/2   G=(dy+1)/2   B=(dz+1)/2 └─▶ VAE encode ──▶ InstantX ControlNet · TRAINED · 1768M └─ 5 residuals ─▶ Qwen-Image-Edit-2509 · FROZEN ──▶ output source photo ────────────────────────────────▶ in-context reference token (carries identity) text embedding = zero — every control signal arrives through the ControlNet

The condition is a single flat colour, so it says which way to face and nothing about where anything is. That is the point: the claim under test is that a large pretrained editing model already holds the head-pose ↔ body-pose prior, and the adapter only has to index it.

How well it follows the condition

Read these with the caveat that the instruments are unreliable. Direction is measured by a head-pose estimator (6DRepNet360), which disagrees with eye-gaze estimators by a median 26.7° on real photographs; id_sim is ArcFace cosine, averaged only over generations where a face was detected at all — which excludes the back-facing cases where identity is hardest. Both this model and its alternative sit near ArcFace's own same-person threshold (≈0.28), so identity preservation is marginal, not solved. The pictures carry more evidence than the numbers.

What is honest to say about this

Zero-shot identities degrade but do not collapse. Large rotations are where both direction accuracy and identity are weakest. Supervision is synthetic — turnaround videos generated with Wan2.2 — which holds identity fixed by construction but bakes that generator's artifacts into the targets. Facial detail is dominated by resolution, not by the model: the same weights and seed render an eye across 25 px or 106 px depending only on framing, a finding that arrived after two rounds of loss engineering had already been spent on it. The repository documents the failures alongside the results.
